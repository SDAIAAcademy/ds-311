The capstone case

Al-Ghadaf Municipality wants a figure it can defend

The requests

2,000 maintenance requests in Arabic, Gulf dialect, English and a mix; five categories with three boundaries to decide.

The tables

The request extract, seven districts with populations, contractors and work orders with costs.

The figure

Requests per 10,000 residents by district and category, and repair cost per contractor.

The same chain as Bayyin's: guideline and agreement, rules and a contract, a gated pipeline, an incident note.

Al-Ghadaf (بلدية الغضف) is fictional; the generator is labs/capstone/make_case.py, seed 2027 by default. The boundaries: a water leak that opened a hole in the road (the reference decision is WATER, the cause), lights inside a park, rubbish inside a park. Teams that decided them by place rather than cause can still score full marks if their rule is explicit and their agreement shows it works.

The repository

What each repository holds

  • A guideline with a change log tied to measured disagreements, and agreement for two pilot rounds.
  • Rules with owners, dimensions, thresholds and severities, and an ODCS contract.
  • A pipeline with a lock file and a CI workflow, run from a clean checkout.
  • Two run logs: one passing on clean data, one stopped by a defect the team seeded, with its incident note.
  • A README reporting agreement and rule results from the two runs, and a datasheet section.

The brief and a README template are in labs/capstone/README.md. Check before the talks that each README has numbers from runs, not plans: the rubric scores results.

How presentations are scored

The rubric

Guideline and agreement
25%
Rules and contract
25%
Pipeline
25%
Incident handling
15%
Reproducibility
10%
Show results from at least two measured runs. A claim without a number is a plan, not a result.

Full marks, one example each. Guideline: the boundary rules are written and the change log cites the alpha that moved. Rules and contract: every rule has an owner, and the contract rejects a renamed column. Pipeline: same bytes twice, quarantine with reasons, nothing published on a block. Incident: the unseen defect caught and traced live. Reproducibility: a clean clone runs with the lock file and CI is green. This rubric is the capstone repository (60% of the course mark); the live review of the seeded defect is scored separately (20%), and the supervised AI-off check makes up the last 20%. Passing: 70% overall and at least 60% on this rubric.

Format

10 minutes for each team, most of it live

1 · Your table · 2'

The figure, your agreement by variety, your rules. One slide or the README.

2 · The seeded defect · 6'

Clone, run on the assessor's extract, show it stop or quarantine, trace it to its rule and rows.

3 · Questions · 2'

Two questions on the guideline and the agreement figures.

Peer feedback. After each talk, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

The seeded defect is one of five: duplicates, district codes, cost units, closed before opened, future dates. Hand the team the extract only when their two minutes end. If their pipeline does not catch it, ask them to find it with a query: tracing still earns part of the incident marks.

The live review

What the assessor looks for in six minutes

→
3 · Stop or set asideby which rule, how many rows
→
4 · Tracerows, cause, affected outputs
The question to answer. "Which figure would have been wrong, and by how much?"

The cost-units defect is the hardest: only February's orders of one contractor are in halalas, and a row rule on a maximum cost may miss the small ones, as A01 did for Bayyin. Teams with a batch-level rule catch it in full. Record which rule caught the defect, or that none did, on the scoring sheet.
